Executive summary

The headline: good calling business, no AI moat

Verdict
TCN has no durable AI moat. Its AI is at competitive parity, built largely on commodity third-party models, and replicable in days. We could not identify a single AI capability TCN offers that its major competitors do not.
  • No edge. Agent assist, summaries, analytics, voicebots, auto-QA. All table stakes, and Five9, NICE and Talkdesk ship them too, often more maturely.
  • Commodity stack. Speech, LLMs and even compliance data are bought, not built. TCN is an integrator, not a model owner.
  • The real moat is elsewhere. Vertical/compliance depth in collections and switching costs. And it is only modest.
  • Behind, not ahead. The only AI-native moat is labor substitution. And TCN's agentic AI had 0 customers and $0 ARR at end-2025 (first customer Apr 2026). AI-native players are built differently. Until TCN converts seats to agents, AI is a feature, not a franchise.
Recommendation. Credit lens
It's a good calling business. Underwrite the loan on that: switching costs, a sticky-ish base, and consumption pricing (AI volume makes them money instead of killing seats). Don't credit the AI story, and treat being behind on AI as a headwind to the credit.

Diligence framework

What would make AI a moat. And how we tested it

A capability is only a moat if a well-funded competitor cannot cheaply replicate it or take the customer. We scored TCN against five tests.

Test 1

Proprietary models / IP

Own models, training pipeline, or technical IP rivals lack.

Test 2

Proprietary data & network effects

Unique data that compounds and improves the product with scale.

Test 3

Workflow lock-in

Switching costs, integrations, data gravity, audit/legal record.

Test 4

Distribution / vertical depth

Channel, brand, and domain depth in a defensible niche.

Test 5. The decisive one

Economic step-change: labor substitution

Does the AI remove the customer's largest cost. Human agents. Rather than merely assist them? This is the only AI capability that re-bases unit economics and creates new lock-in. We weight it highest.

Methodology: public materials & competitor disclosures · review corpora (G2/Capterra) · third-party headcount/financial estimates · hands-on reconstruction of TCN's AI components.

The decisive question

The only AI moat is labor substitution

Contact-center cost stack
~60-70%
is human agent labor. The prize

Augmentation (today)

Copilots & summaries trim handle time. Incremental. And equally available to every competitor. No lock-in.

Substitution (the moat)

Replace seats with autonomous agents → re-bases unit economics, shifts pricing seat→outcome, and creates an automated-resolution data flywheel + deep lock-in.

Thesis
A moat is created only by convincing customers to remove human agents and replace them with AI agents. Industry-wide that conversion has not happened at scale. "agentic" is still largely demoware. The first mover to convert seats into agents captures the labor budget and the lock-in. Until TCN does this, there is no moat.

TCN-specific angle

Collections is unusually automatable. Scripted, repetitive, compliance-bounded. So the opportunity is genuine. But TCN sells augmentation today and is sub-scale on R&D. This is the single most important item to diligence and to fund.

Why they're behind

TCN builds AI for the human, not the agent

What TCN does

Adds AI to the human's workflow: agent assist, call summaries, QA scoring, better answering-machine detection. Useful. But a human is still on the call. It's incremental.

What the AI-natives do

Rethink the whole process. The AI runs the call end to end, no human, starting with one narrow workflow and expanding. That's how you actually take the labor budget.

Two problems
Labor dollars: TCN keeps saying it'll win them. It hasn't picked up any yet. Talent: leadership is the Bird family (Terrel, Jesse, Darrin) plus a 30-year software vet, all deep contact-center. I don't see AI/ML research leadership or named AI hires. The CIP claims "AI talent." Where is it? If they were AI-native they wouldn't be this far behind.

The call

They've got a good platform. Double down, AI-enable it, and move a lot faster. Right now they're behind, and the people who aren't behind can walk into their accounts.

My expectation: what I'd build

If I ran AI at TCN, here's what I'd build

Kathy Slowinski, if I ran AI/product here: I wouldn't bolt bots onto human calls. I'd take one workflow, make the AI run it end to end, keep it on TCN's phone lines, then expand. Roughly:

Now · 0-6 mo

Pick one workflow, own the line

Take one high-volume, scriptable workflow (first-party reminders, inbound payment, promise-to-pay). Stand up a forward-deployed team to build it. Default it onto TCN's own telephony so the AI call stays on TCN's pipes. Track containment, cost-per-resolution and liquidation from day one.

Next · 6-18 mo

Run it end to end, price on outcomes

Make that workflow fully autonomous, no human, compliance in the loop. Charge per resolution, not per seat. Publish containment and recovery numbers with named customers. Lock the phone line into the contract.

Later · 18-36 mo

Make it most of the volume

Automated resolutions become the majority of calls. Use that data to keep getting better (the flywheel). Expand workflow by workflow, then into healthcare RCM. Defend with compliance and the audit record.

The test
It's not "do you have AI features." It's "can the AI run the call end to end and keep the phone line on TCN." That's the whole game, and it's exactly what the AI-natives are already doing.

What I'd do

Underwrite the calling business, not the AI story

  • The cash flows are real. They come from the vertical and switching costs, not the AI. That's what services the debt.
  • Don't credit the AI. It's slideware today, and being this far behind makes AI a headwind to the credit, not upside.
  • Downside's contained by sticky-ish collections workflows and the audit records. Good for a credit.
  • If we lend: monitor dial-path volume and AI-native displacement in the base, and push them to run one workflow end to end and keep the phone line. That's their only path to a real moat.
  • Risks: too small on R&D, CCaaS going commodity, big players bundling agents + compliance, and the AI-natives taking the call.
Bottom line
Good niche calling business, modest moat from the vertical and switching costs, no AI moat today. The AI only matters if they actually replace human agents, and they haven't started. Price what's real. Treat the AI as an option, not the reason to buy.

Preliminary view for BSP

Is there something here? Yes, a "vertical Five9"

Leon's framing is right. TCN is a collections/ARM Five9: same CCaaS, usage-based economics, in a narrower and more defensible vertical. The question is what that's worth.

Like Five9

Cloud dialer + omnichannel CCaaS. Usage-exposed revenue. AI sold as an add-on. And like Five9, exposed to AI-native disruption of the seat model.

TCN's edge vs. Five9

Collections/compliance depth. Consumption pricing, not per-seat, so AI adds revenue instead of killing seats. Last independent standing after LiveVox went to NICE. Bootstrapped and profitable.

TCN's disadvantage

~17x smaller than Five9, so far less AI R&D. Thinner enterprise footprint. And a "vertical Five9" faces the same agentic disruption with less money to fight it.

Verdict
Worth pursuing. A real, defensible niche business (clears Rule of 40, sticky-ish, consumption-priced). But the moat is the vertical, switching costs and the pricing model, not AI. Underwrite it as a lender on the existing business; the future business is slideware. The AI headwind is the thing the management call has to test.

Recommended next step

Management-call topics to validate the moat

Proposed topics list for Union Square. Designed to confirm whether the moat is real, whether AI is traction or narrative, and whether TCN can build a durable moat.

1 · Revenue durability

GRR is 81% while switching costs are "deep". What drives the ~19% gross churn/downsell, and in which segment? How much ARR is volume-variable vs. committed minimums (downside in a soft quarter)?

2 · AI traction, not narrative

AI Voice Agent: live customer count, ARR, containment/automation rate and cost-per-resolution today? Do agent calls run on TCN's telephony, or can customers bring their own? (dial-path retention)

3 · Proprietary AI

"In-house model training for Virtual Agents". Trained model, fine-tune, or prompting on GPT/Gemini? What is actually owned beyond SmartAMD? Realized AI ARPU vs. the "4-10× human-minute" claim?

4 · Competition / disintermediation

How often do Skit.ai / Decagon / Sierra / Convoso appear in deals, and the trend? Any volume lost to an AI-native? Post-LiveVox/NICE displacement. Win-rate vs. Five9 in collections, and how durable?

5 · Compliance depth

Is the consent/audit ledger a court-tested, sellable differentiator or configurable rules like peers. Any case where TCN's records were the legal defense? Cost-to-serve to stay current across 50 states + federal + intl?

6 · Scale, economics & strategy

R&D as % of revenue vs. Five9/NICE and the funded AI-natives; does AI expand or compress gross margin? 3-yr plan and targets to convert seats→agents; capital / M&A / partnership posture to lead substitution?
