# Fund Outreach Email Templates

Tight, copy-paste templates for the 8-min-per-touch math. Each one is
one screen, one ask, one next step. Edit the `{{placeholders}}`, hit send.

**Style rules:**
- Subject lines are short and specific. No "Quick question" / "Touching base".
- One ask per email. Specific next step with a real time window.
- No multi-paragraph context dumps. They can ask for the deck.
- Sign off the same way every time. Don't reinvent the closing.

---

## 1. Cold — never met, no intro

**Subject:** `{{Fund}} — {{your edge in 5 words}}`

> Hi {{First}},
>
> I'm {{Your Name}}, {{role}} at {{Fund}}. We're a {{stage}} fund focused
> on {{thesis in one line}} — {{one concrete proof point: portfolio name,
> recent exit, fund size, traction stat}}.
>
> I reached out because {{specific reason this LP fits — check size,
> mandate, prior bets in your space, something they said publicly}}.
>
> Worth a 15-minute intro call in the next two weeks? I have
> {{Day, time}} or {{Day, time}} open — happy to work around you.
>
> — {{Your Name}}

**Why it works:** 6 lines. The middle line is the only one that takes
real thought — the rest is reusable. If you can't name a specific reason
this LP fits, don't send it; go back to sourcing.

---

## 2. Warm — asking an existing contact for an intro

**Subject:** `Quick ask — intro to {{Target}} at {{Their Firm}}?`

> Hey {{First}},
>
> Hope you're well. Quick ask: would you be open to introducing me to
> {{Target}} at {{Their Firm}}? I noticed {{how you know they're
> connected — LinkedIn, a portfolio overlap, something they mentioned}}.
>
> We're raising {{Fund N}} ({{size}}, {{thesis one-liner}}) and
> {{Their Firm}} looks like a strong fit because {{one sentence}}.
>
> Happy to send a forwardable blurb if it's easier — just say the word
> and I'll have it to you today.
>
> Thanks either way,
> {{Your Name}}

**Why it works:** Gives them an easy out ("either way") and an easy yes
("just say the word"). Always offer the forwardable — most warm intros
die because the asker makes the connector do the writing.

---

## 3. Forwardable blurb (paste into #2 when asked)

> {{Target First}} — meet {{Your Name}}, {{role}} at {{Fund}}. They're
> raising {{Fund N}}, a {{size}} {{stage}} fund focused on {{thesis}}.
> Recent wins: {{1-2 portfolio names or proof points}}. They thought
> {{Their Firm}} could be a good fit because {{one sentence}}. I'll let
> you two take it from here.

**Why it works:** The connector can forward verbatim. No editing tax on
them = higher send rate.

---

## 4. Post-intro — connector has made the intro

**Subject:** reply to the intro thread, don't start a new one

> {{Connector}}, thanks for the intro — moving you to bcc.
>
> {{Target}}, great to meet you. As {{Connector}} mentioned, we're
> {{thesis one-liner}}. The reason I was excited to connect: {{specific
> reason — their fund mandate, a portfolio overlap, something they've
> said publicly}}.
>
> Would a 20-minute call in the next two weeks make sense? I have
> {{Day, time}} or {{Day, time}} — or send me a Calendly link if easier.
>
> — {{Your Name}}

**Why it works:** Moving the connector to bcc is the move — keeps the
thread clean and signals you respect their inbox. Specific time slots
beat "let me know when works."

---

## 5. Soft nudge — no reply, 7–10 days out

**Subject:** reply on the original thread, don't start a new one

> Hi {{First}} — bumping this in case it got buried. Still keen to find
> 15 minutes if you're open. Otherwise, all good — feel free to tell me
> to follow up next quarter.
>
> — {{Your Name}}

**Why it works:** Two sentences. Gives them a graceful "later" option,
which gets more replies than a hard yes/no. Same thread so they have
context without re-reading anything.

---

## 6. Harder nudge — no reply, ~3 weeks

**Subject:** reply on the original thread

> {{First}}, last nudge from me — adding one data point that might be
> relevant: {{something new and concrete: a portfolio milestone, a
> recent deal, a new LP signed, a press hit}}.
>
> If now isn't the moment, no problem. Want me to circle back in
> {{Q+1}}?
>
> — {{Your Name}}

**Why it works:** Gives them a reason to re-engage (the new data point)
without re-pitching the whole fund. The "circle back next quarter"
question is easy to answer with one word.

---

## 7. Close-the-loop — no reply, 6+ weeks

**Subject:** reply on the original thread

> {{First}} — I'll stop bugging your inbox after this. If you'd like me
> to take you off the list, just reply "pass" and I will. Otherwise I'll
> assume timing and check back in {{Q+1}}.
>
> Appreciate you,
> {{Your Name}}

**Why it works:** Permission-to-leave emails get surprisingly high reply
rates because they remove all social cost. You'll get a clean "pass," a
"sorry, slammed — Q+1 works," or a meeting. All three are wins compared
to silence.

---

## 8. Post-meeting follow-up — same day, within 4 hours

**Subject:** `Following up — {{Fund}} / {{Their Firm}}`

> {{First}}, thanks for the time today. Quick recap of what I heard:
>
> - {{Their stated mandate / check size}}
> - {{What they want to see next: data room, references, deck v2, etc.}}
> - {{Their stated next step / timeline}}
>
> Attached: {{deck / DDQ / track record / whatever they asked for}}.
>
> I'll plan to follow up {{specific date}} unless I hear from you sooner.
>
> — {{Your Name}}

**Why it works:** The recap forces them to correct you if you got it
wrong — which means you find out *now* instead of two weeks from now.
The specific follow-up date prevents the thread from going dark.

---

## 9. "Check back later" — they said {{Q+1}}, it's {{Q+1}}

**Subject:** `As promised — {{Q}} check-in`

> Hi {{First}}, you mentioned {{this quarter}} might be a better time.
> Quick update on where we are:
>
> - {{New milestone 1}}
> - {{New milestone 2}}
> - {{Where the raise stands: $X committed, target close date}}
>
> Still keen to find 20 minutes if the timing has gotten better. I have
> {{Day, time}} or {{Day, time}} — or send me a link.
>
> — {{Your Name}}

**Why it works:** "As promised" + a real update earns the open. Three
bullets is the right amount — enough to show momentum, not so much that
they'd rather read it later.

---

## 10. Referral ask — to an existing LP or warm contact

**Subject:** `One favor — {{Fund N}} referrals`

> Hey {{First}}, hope {{their last big thing}} is going well.
>
> Quick favor: we're {{$X}} into {{Fund N}}'s raise with a {{target
> close date}} target close. The single highest-leverage thing for me
> right now is warm intros to other LPs who fit our profile
> ({{size, type, thesis fit}}).
>
> Anyone come to mind? Even one or two names would help a lot. Happy to
> send a forwardable blurb to make it zero-effort on your end.
>
> Thanks,
> {{Your Name}}

**Why it works:** Frames the ask as small ("one or two"), credits the
existing relationship ("highest-leverage thing for me"), and pre-empts
the friction with the forwardable offer.

| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Cold            | `{{Fund}} — {{edge in 5 words}}`               |
| Warm ask        | `Quick ask — intro to {{Target}}?`             |
| Post-intro      | reply on intro thread (no new subject)         |
| Any nudge       | reply on original thread (no new subject)      |
| Post-meeting    | `Following up — {{Fund}} / {{Their Firm}}`     |
| Re-engage later | `As promised — {{Q}} check-in`                 |
| Referral ask    | `One favor — {{Fund N}} referrals`             |

## What never to write

- "Just circling back" / "Just checking in" — empty calories, no new info
- "I know you're busy" — everyone is, don't lead with their excuse
- "I'd love to pick your brain" — vague, low-status, opt-out trigger
- "Synergy" / "alignment" / "value-add" — VC mad-libs, instant skim
- Multi-paragraph fund history — they'll ask for the deck if they want it
- Calendly link with no proposed times — make them counter, not search
